Marketplace Merchant Content Review Guidelines

Last updated 10 November 2026

The Maestro Pro Marketplace lets musicians sell sheet music, backing tracks, karaoke, lyrics and produced music to other Maestro Pro players. We want it to be a place where composers, arrangers and performers are paid fairly for their own work, and where buyers can trust what they download.

These guidelines apply to every merchant and every item sold on the Marketplace. By opening a Merchant Account or submitting content you agree to them. They are operated by The Caffeine Media Company ("we", "us"), the company behind Maestro Pro.

Every item is checked before it can go on sale. If you are unsure whether something is allowed, assume it needs your own permission, and ask us at support@maestro-pro.app before you submit it.

1. Safety and file integrity

1.1 No harmful files

Every file in every submission is scanned with VirusTotal before it is reviewed. Content that contains malware, scripts, executables, or anything designed to damage or gain access to a device, or that any scanning engine reports as malicious or suspicious, is refused.

1.2 Permitted file types

A submission is a Maestro Pro bundle that contains only sheet music (MusicXML, MXL, XML, PDF), MIDI or karaoke (MID, MIDI, KAR, CDG), lyrics (LRC, TXT) and backing recordings (MP3, M4A, WAV, AIF, AIFF, CAF, AAC, FLAC). Bundles with any other kind of file, with disguised file types, or with a damaged layout are refused.

1.3 Objectionable content

Content must not be unlawful, hateful, defamatory, sexually explicit, or an incitement to violence, and must not contain other people's personal data. Lyrics and titles are included in this rule.

2. Ownership and copyright

This is the most important section. Selling music that belongs to someone else, without their permission, harms the people who made it and is not allowed here.

2.1 You must have the right to sell it

You may only submit content that you created yourself, or that you have the legal right to sell to the public. By submitting an item you confirm that you are the owner of the rights in all of it, or hold a written licence that allows you to sell it, and that you can show us that licence if we ask.

2.2 Arrangements, transcriptions and covers of protected works

A new arrangement, transcription, tab, reduction or note-for-note copy of a song or composition that is still in copyright is a copy of the original and needs permission from the copyright owner (usually the publisher). Changing the key, instrumentation or layout does not remove that need. Without a licence, these items are refused.

2.3 Public domain works

You may sell your own new engraving, arrangement or edition of a work that is in the public domain in the United Kingdom, the European Union and the United States. You may not copy, scan or convert another publisher's edition, including one from a library, website or print book, because the edition itself may still be protected. A work is not in the public domain just because it is old, free to download, or has no copyright notice.

2.4 Recordings and backing tracks

Backing tracks must be recordings you made or produced yourself from material you have the right to use. You may not sell, re-upload or re-record commercial releases, ripped or extracted audio, sampled recordings, karaoke tracks made by others, or voice or sound clones of real artists. A cover recording of a protected song needs the licences that song requires.

2.5 Lyrics

Lyrics are protected on their own, even when the music is not. You may only include lyrics that you wrote, or that you are licensed to distribute, or that are in the public domain.

2.6 Karaoke and CD+G

Karaoke files (KAR, CDG) must be your own productions of material you may use. Files taken from commercial karaoke discs, or from karaoke packs made by other people, are refused.

2.7 Names, trademarks and credit

You must not use another person's name, band, label or trademark in a way that suggests the item is theirs, endorsed by them, or the official version. Composers, arrangers and original writers must be credited accurately in the listing.

2.8 Content made with AI tools

Content made with the help of AI tools is allowed only if you have the right to sell the result, and it does not copy or closely imitate an existing protected work, or the voice or style of a particular living artist.

2.9 Where an item is sold

You choose, item by item, whether it is sold worldwide or only in the countries you list. You must only make an item available in countries where you hold the rights to sell it. A work that is public domain in one country may still be protected in another, and a licence may cover only some territories.

2.10 Proof of ownership

You can upload supporting documents with an item, such as a licence, a letter of permission from the rights holder, a copyright registration, or dated drafts and project files that show you wrote it. The documents are scanned with VirusTotal and read by the reviewer. They make a refusal less likely, but they are not a guarantee, and they do not move your responsibility to us (9.2).

3. Listings and pricing

3.1 Accurate listings

The title, composer, description and list of contents must describe what is really in the bundle. Listings must not be misleading, contain advertising for other services, or be copied from other people's pages.

3.2 Quality and duplicates

Items must open and play in Maestro Pro. Empty bundles, test uploads, near-identical duplicates of your own items, and bundles that are mainly padding are refused.

3.3 Prices

Prices are set in US dollars, from $1.00 to $999.75, and must end in .00, .25, .50 or .75. No other cent amount is allowed. Buyers are charged in their own currency at the time of purchase.

3.4 Versions

Different keys, instruments or arrangements of the same piece belong in one item as versions, not in separate items. A buyer gets every version in the item. Each version must be named so a buyer can tell them apart, for example the key or the instrument, and every version must be your own work or covered by the same rights.

3.5 Produced music: singles, EPs and albums

You may sell your own recordings as a single (1 to 3 tracks), an EP or an album. The recordings must be yours. A cover, remix or sample of a published song or recording needs a licence from the rights holders of the song and of any recording used (2.2 to 2.5). Every track needs a clear title, the listing must say truthfully whether it is a single, an EP or an album (11.1), and any cover art must be yours too. Buyers receive the audio files and may keep them for their own listening (6.3).

3.6 Sales tax, VAT and GST

Where the law of the buyer's country requires it, tax is charged on a sale: VAT, GST or a similar tax. Where buyers expect prices to include tax, the price shown on the Marketplace already includes it, and the checkout shows the price before tax, the tax and the total. Elsewhere the tax is added at checkout. The price you set is the price before tax. Your share of a sale is worked out on that price, never on the tax. Maestro Pro collects the tax from the buyer and pays it to the tax authorities. If a sale is refunded, its tax is refunded with it.

4. Merchant Accounts and fees

4.1 Technology fee

A Merchant Account needs a one-time technology fee of £5, paid by card or another method offered at checkout, before you can add content. The fee covers setting up your account and is not repeated. It does not affect any rights you have under consumer law.

4.2 Payouts and the marketplace share

Sales are paid through Mollie. You must connect a Mollie account for payouts before you can add content. We keep 30% of every sale price as the marketplace share, and the rest is paid to your Mollie account. Mollie's own terms, checks and fees apply to your account. Mollie's fee for a card payment (currently 3.25% + £0.20) is built into the price the buyer pays, which is rounded to the nearest 25 cents, so it does not come out of your share: the Marketplace's 30% is taken on the price you set, and you receive the rest, including what covers Mollie's fee. Mollie's other terms and checks still apply to your account.

4.3 Your details

The name and email address on your Merchant Account must be accurate, and you may hold only one Merchant Account. You are responsible for keeping your sign-in link private, and for the taxes due on your earnings.

4.4 Taxes are yours

All tax obligations that arise from your sales and earnings are yours alone, and none are ours. That includes income and corporation tax, VAT, GST and sales tax, any duty to register for, charge, collect, report or pay tax in any country, and any penalties or interest. Maestro Pro and Caffeine Media do not collect, report or pay tax on your behalf, do not give tax advice, and are not responsible if you fail to meet a tax obligation. The marketplace share is taken from the sale price and is not tax advice or a tax payment. You are responsible for finding out what applies to you.

4.5 Closing your account

You can close your Merchant Account at any time from the Account tab. Your content comes off the Marketplace, and anything that never went on sale is deleted. Buyers who already bought your items keep their downloads. We keep records of orders, sales, tax and refunds, and your responsibilities under these guidelines (including refunds, copyright claims and taxes) continue after closing. We email you to confirm. A closed account can't be used or replaced for a month. After that, signing up again with the same email address offers to reopen the same account for a £15 technology fee. Anything you had for sale stays off sale until it has been reviewed again. If you reopen an account and close it again, the closure is permanent: it can never be reopened, and the email address can't be used for another Merchant Account.

4.6 What buyers see on their statement

Sales are paid to your own Mollie account, so your business details, not Maestro Pro's, appear on the buyer's bank or card statement. Set your Mollie business name to something buyers will recognise, and use maestro-pro.app as your business website in your Mollie profile. A buyer who does not recognise a charge may dispute it with their bank, and that is your responsibility (9.2).

5. The review process

5.1 Every item is reviewed

No item can be sold until it has been reviewed. Review has two automatic steps: every file is scanned with VirusTotal, and then an automated reviewer checks the item against these guidelines.

5.2 Outcomes

An item is either Accepted, which makes it Ready For Distribution and visible on the Marketplace, or Refused. A refusal always comes with a reason and a link to the guideline it breaks. You can fix the problem and submit the item again.

5.3 Mistakes and appeals

Automated review can be wrong. If an item is refused you can appeal from your Merchant Account: explain your case and upload supporting evidence, such as a licence, a letter of permission or proof that you wrote the work. Every evidence file is scanned with VirusTotal, and then the item is assessed again with your evidence in front of the reviewer. If an item has been refused three times, it is passed to a person at Maestro Pro, who makes a final decision to accept it or to refuse it permanently. An item being accepted does not mean we have checked that you own it, and it does not move your responsibility to us.

5.4 Review at any time

We may review any item again at any time, including after it has been accepted, for example after a complaint or a change in these guidelines.

5.5 Editing an item

You can edit an item at any time. The whole item, not only the change, is scanned and reviewed again, and it is off sale until it is accepted. Buyers who already bought the item keep the last accepted version while the edit is reviewed, and get the new version once it is accepted. Editing a refused item does not reset the number of times it has been refused.

6. Buyers keep what they buy

6.1 Purchased content stays with the buyer

Once a buyer has paid for an item, they can always download it again, unless the purchase has been refunded under section 7. You cannot take purchased content away from the people who paid for it. Withdrawing an item from sale stops new sales only. Choosing the countries where an item is sold affects new sales only.

6.2 Narrow exceptions

We will only stop a buyer downloading an item they paid for where the law or a court requires it, or where the file turns out to be harmful to their device. If we do, the buyer is refunded.

6.3 Downloads and personal use

Every file in an item can be downloaded by the buyer, including printable PDFs, and used outside Maestro Pro for their own personal use: to play, practise and print a copy for themselves. By selling an item the merchant gives the buyer this personal-use licence. Files must not be locked, and buyers may not resell or share them.

7. Refunds and cancellation

These rules protect buyers, and they apply to every merchant. Merchants agree to them when they open a Merchant Account.

7.1 The 14-day right to cancel

A buyer may withdraw from a purchase and receive a full refund at any time within 14 days of paying for it. The buyer does not have to give a reason, and it makes no difference whether they have already downloaded the item.

7.2 No refund after buying again

The right to cancel is lost if the buyer has already had a refund for the same item from the same merchant and then buys it again. A purchase made after a refund of the same item cannot be refunded under this section.

7.3 Refunds are paid by the merchant

A refund is taken from the merchant's Mollie account, as the seller of the item. The merchant is charged the entire value of the refund, including the 30% marketplace share that Maestro Pro kept from the sale, and must also pay the refund fee that Mollie charges for processing it. A merchant cannot refuse, delay or reduce a refund that is due under section 7.

7.4 What a refund does

When a purchase is refunded, the buyer's access to that purchase ends. In the merchant's earnings the sale is replaced by a charge for the full refund, so a refunded sale costs the merchant the marketplace share as well. A buyer who needs the item again must buy it again.

7.5 If a merchant cannot cover a refund

If a refund cannot be paid from the merchant's Mollie account, the merchant remains responsible for it. We may hold the merchant's payouts, withdraw their items from sale, or close their account until it is paid. Statutory rights of buyers are not affected by anything in these guidelines.

8. Copyright disputes

8.1 Dispute Resolution Centre

If you own the rights to something sold on the Marketplace and you did not allow it, file a dispute in the Dispute Resolution Centre on the Marketplace website, or tell Maestro Pro Support, who can file it for you. Say which work is yours and why the item infringes it. A claim must be made in good faith. Claims that are false or made to harm a merchant are dismissed, and the person making them may be blocked.

8.2 What happens to the item

While a dispute is open the item is off sale. The merchant is told, and has 7 days to respond in the Dispute Resolution Centre with their own evidence, such as a licence or proof that they wrote the work. We may decide without a response after 7 days. A person at Maestro Pro decides every dispute. If it is dismissed the item goes back on sale. People who already bought the item keep it (6.1).

8.3 Copyright strikes

A dispute that is upheld removes the item for good and gives the merchant a copyright strike. Merchants who sell content they do not have the right to sell are warned, and strikes stay on the Merchant Account.

8.4 Three strikes

A merchant with 3 copyright strikes is barred from the Marketplace. The Merchant Account is closed, every item is withdrawn from sale, payouts connected with the account are cancelled, our connection to the merchant's payment account is cut, and no technology fee is refunded. Money that has already been paid into the merchant's own payment account is between the merchant and their payment provider, and the merchant remains responsible for any refunds that are due (7.3).

8.5 Appeals

Either side can appeal a decision once, within 14 days of it. A merchant can appeal an upheld dispute, and a rights holder can appeal a dismissed one. An appeal must include evidence: a statement and at least one file, such as a licence, a letter of permission, a registration or proof of authorship. A person at Maestro Pro looks at the appeal again. If a merchant's appeal against an upheld dispute fails, the decision stands and the merchant does not get another copyright strike. If it succeeds, the copyright strike is removed, the item goes back on sale and, if the strike had closed the Merchant Account, the account is reopened (the merchant must reconnect their payment account). If a rights holder's appeal against a dismissed dispute succeeds, the dispute is upheld: the item is removed for good and the merchant gets a copyright strike. If it fails, the item stays on sale.

9. Legal terms for merchants

9.1 Your promises

You promise that everything you submit is yours to sell, that it does not infringe anyone's copyright, trademark, privacy or other rights, that the details you give are true, and that you have followed these guidelines.

9.2 You are the seller, and you are responsible

The Marketplace is a platform that hosts and delivers content on behalf of merchants. Merchants, not Maestro Pro or Caffeine Media, are the creators and sellers of the content. The merchant takes all legal and financial responsibility for what they sell and for who they sell it to. That includes the content and the rights in it, the countries where it is offered, claims and complaints from buyers or rights holders, refunds, all taxes such as income tax, VAT and sales tax (see 4.4), compliance with consumer, sanctions and export laws, and any loss, cost or liability that arises from a sale. We do not check ownership of every item, we do not check who buys, and we do not endorse any item.

9.3 Covering our costs

You will cover any loss, damages, penalties and reasonable legal costs that we suffer because of a claim that something you sold infringes someone's rights, or because you broke these guidelines.

9.4 Permission to host and deliver

You give us a non-exclusive licence to store, copy, scan, review (including with automated systems), display and deliver your content and listing to buyers for as long as it is on the Marketplace, and for as long as a buyer is entitled to download it. You keep all other rights in your work.

9.5 Limits on our liability

As far as the law allows, we are not liable for the loss of sales, income or data, or for indirect loss, arising from the use of the Marketplace. Nothing in these guidelines limits liability that cannot be limited by law.

9.6 Governing law

These guidelines are governed by the law of England and Wales, and the courts of England and Wales have exclusive jurisdiction, apart from any rights that a consumer has to bring a claim in their home country.

9.7 Changes

We may change these guidelines. The date at the top shows the latest version, and new items are reviewed against the version in force on the day of review.

10. Spam

The Marketplace should be easy to browse. Spam makes it harder for buyers to find good music and for honest merchants to be found.

10.1 Repeated uploads

Do not upload the same music more than once. The same piece in another key, for another instrument or in another arrangement belongs in one item as a version (3.4). An item that contains a file identical to a file in another of your items is refused.

10.2 Flooding and padding

Do not flood the Marketplace with large numbers of near-identical, low-effort, automatically generated or padded items, or with items made only to gain search position or sales. We may refuse the items and limit how many you can submit.

10.3 Advertising and keyword stuffing

Titles, descriptions, version names and file contents must not be used to advertise other products, services or websites, or to repeat words to appear in more searches.

11. Misleading or Confusing Consumers

A buyer should know exactly what they are buying before they pay.

11.1 Say what it is

The title, composer, description, version names, price and contents must say clearly what a buyer gets: the piece, the key or instrument of each version, whether it is sheet music, a backing track, karaoke or lyrics, and whether it is a full or a partial arrangement.

11.2 No false claims

Do not claim an item is official, endorsed, licensed or made by someone it is not. Do not use another artist's, composer's or publisher's name, logo or title to suggest a link. Do not describe a work as original, public domain or free of rights unless it is.

11.3 No bait and no hidden limits

Do not give an item a title or description made to be mistaken for a different, more famous work, composer or product. Do not hide limits such as a shortened piece, missing parts, a poor recording, or being sold in only some countries.

11.4 Fair pricing

A preview, a sample or one part of a piece must not be sold at the price of the whole piece without saying so, and a price must not be set to confuse, for example by a version name that suggests a bundle that is not included.

12. Enforcement

12.1 What can happen

If you break these guidelines we may refuse an item, withdraw it from sale, hold payouts connected with it, suspend your Merchant Account, or close it. How strongly we act depends on how serious the breach is, whether it was deliberate, and whether it has happened before.
